# Glossary

Accuracy
: The average variation in the bias between measurements and truth. Good
  measurements are both accurate and precise.

Ammeter
: A device that measures electrical current.

{ref}`Bandwidth <theory-bandwidth>`
: The frequency at which a measurement's gain falls to −3 dB, about 71 % of
  the input amplitude.  Lower bandwidth spreads short events out in time and
  lowers their peaks, but does not change their charge or energy.

Burden Voltage
: The voltage drop that occurs through the test equipment device. For shunt
  ammeters, the burden voltage includes the voltage across the shunt
  resistor, connectors, fuses, and any additional resistance in the device.

Current
: The flow of electric charge per time from positive voltage to negative
  voltage, measured in amperes (A), often called amps. One amp is one coulomb
  of charge flowing per second. One coulomb is approximately 6.242 x 10¹⁸
  electrons, but electric current is defined opposite to the electron flow.

Energy
: The total power exerted over time, measured in joules (J). Energy is the
  time integral of power.

{ref}`Ground Loop <theory-ground-loops>`
: A closed path formed by ground connections, such as a computer connected
  by USB to both a Joulescope and a debugger that share the target's ground.
  Ground loops cause noise and can let current bypass the current meter.

Insertion Loss
: The loss in signal power due to a device in the signal path, most commonly
  used in reference to radio frequency (RF) or telecommunications. See
  **Burden Voltage**.

{ref}`Leakage Current <theory-leakage>`
: Current that flows through equipment connected to the target, such as a
  debugger, logic analyzer or oscilloscope, rather than through the target
  itself.  Leakage adds error to low-current measurements.

Multimeter
: A multifunction device that usually incorporates a voltmeter and shunt
  ammeter, at a minimum. A standard multimeter supports only one measurement
  quantity at a time.

Ohm’s Law
: The equation that relates voltage, current and resistance: V = I \* R

Oscilloscope
: A device that graphically displays one or more signals, usually voltages,
  varying over time as a two-dimensional plot with time on the x-axis and the
  signal on the y-axis.

Power
: The time-rate of doing work, measured in watts (W). Power is voltage
  multiplied by current.

Precision
: The amount of variation between measurements of the same value relative to
  each other. Good measurements are both accurate and precise.

Resistance
: The quantity of opposition to the flow of electric current.

Shunt Ammeter
: An ammeter that measures the voltage across a resistor, and then uses Ohm’s
  Law to compute current.

Shunt Resistor
: The resistor used by “Shunt Ammeters” to detect current using Ohm’s Law:
  I = V / R. Given a known R and a measured V, the ammeter computes I.

Universal Serial Bus (USB)
: The now ubiquitous standard for connecting computers and peripherals.
  Joulescope connects to the host computer using USB 2.0 high-speed operating
  at 480 Mbps.

Voltage
: The electric potential difference, measured in volts (V).

Voltmeter
: A device that measures electrical voltage.
